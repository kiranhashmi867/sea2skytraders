Dry Fruits

5 ways to tell premium almonds from ordinary ones

September 20262 min read

Not all almonds are created equal. Whether you are buying a single pack or ordering in bulk, here is what to look for the next time you choose a batch.

What to check

  1. Colour: A uniform light brown skin usually signals proper drying and storage.
  2. Size: Larger, evenly sized almonds tend to come from better growing conditions.
  3. Crunch: A fresh almond snaps cleanly rather than feeling soft or chewy.
  4. Smell: Premium almonds have a mild, nutty aroma with no rancid or musty notes.
  5. Packaging: Resealable packs that block moisture and air keep almonds fresher for longer.

Keeping them fresh at home

Almonds contain natural oils, and heat, light and air are what make them go stale. Keep them in an airtight container in a cool, dark cupboard, and move them to the fridge if you will not finish the pack within a few weeks.

Why dates make a great everyday snack

June 20262 min read

Dates are a pantry staple in households across the world, and it is easy to see why.

  • Naturally sweet with no added sugar
  • A convenient, ready-to-eat snack
  • A soft texture that is easy to enjoy on its own or in recipes
  • A long shelf life when stored properly

Five easy ways to enjoy them

  1. Stuffed: Remove the stone and fill each date with an almond or a walnut half.
  2. At breakfast: Chop them into porridge, yoghurt or cereal.
  3. In smoothies: Blend one or two in for natural sweetness.
  4. In baking: Use chopped dates or date paste in place of some of the sugar.
  5. With tea or coffee: A classic pairing in many countries.

A sensible portion

Dates are a source of fibre, but they are also naturally high in sugar, so a few at a time is plenty for most people.

Dry Fruits

Almonds, pistachios, walnuts, cashews and raisins: which one for what?

October 20262 min read

They often sit side by side in the same bowl, but each dry fruit has its own flavour, texture and best use. Here is a quick guide to choosing the right one.

Almonds

Firm, crunchy and mildly sweet. Good for snacking, and they hold their shape when sliced over rice dishes, desserts and salads.

Pistachios

Slightly sweet with a rich, distinctive flavour, and a green colour that looks beautiful as a garnish. A favourite in desserts, ice cream and baking.

Walnuts

Softer, with an earthy and slightly bitter note. They pair well with honey, cheese and fruit, and are a classic in cakes and brownies.

Cashews

Buttery and creamy. Great roasted as a snack, and they blend into smooth sauces and curries.

Raisins

Chewy and sweet. Use them in baking, rice dishes, breakfast cereal or a simple trail mix.

A simple way to serve them

A small handful of mixed nuts with a few raisins makes an easy snack. Nuts provide protein, fibre and unsaturated fats, but they are also high in calories, so a handful, about 30g, is a common serving.

Dry Fruits

How to store dry fruits so they stay fresh for months

October 20262 min read

Good dry fruits are worth looking after. Stored well, they keep their flavour and crunch for months. Stored badly, nuts turn stale and bitter surprisingly fast.

Why nuts go off

Nuts are rich in natural oils. Heat, light and air cause those oils to turn rancid, which gives a sharp, bitter smell and taste.

Four rules that work

  1. Keep them airtight: Use a jar or a resealable pack, and squeeze the air out before closing.
  2. Keep them cool: A cupboard away from the cooker is fine for a few weeks.
  3. Keep them dark: Light speeds up staleness, so avoid clear jars on a sunny shelf.
  4. Keep them dry: Moisture leads to mould, so always use a dry spoon or dry hands.

For longer storage

For anything you will not use within a month or two, the fridge is better, and the freezer keeps nuts well for much longer. Nuts absorb smells, so keep them sealed and away from onions, spices and strong cheeses.

Dates and raisins

Dried fruits like dates and raisins keep best in an airtight container too. If white specks appear on dates, it is usually natural sugar coming to the surface, not mould. Mould looks fuzzy and smells off.

If nuts smell sharp or taste harsh, they have turned. Trust your nose.

Pink Salt

7 everyday uses for Himalayan pink salt

August 20262 min read

Pink salt is far more versatile than most people realise. Here are a few ways our customers use it beyond the dinner table.

What it is

Himalayan pink salt is a rock salt mined in the Punjab region of Pakistan. Its pink colour comes from trace minerals in the rock. Like any salt it is mostly sodium chloride, so in the kitchen you can use it just as you would use regular salt.

Seven ways to use it

  1. Everyday cooking and seasoning: Fine grain dissolves quickly in food.
  2. Relaxing bath soaks: Dissolve a handful in warm water.
  3. Homemade scrubs: Mix fine salt with a little oil.
  4. Decorative salt lamps: For soft, ambient lighting.
  5. Natural home décor accents: Chunks look good in bowls and on shelves.
  6. Gifting: Fill small decorative jars.
  7. Craft and DIY home projects.

Good to know: unlike most table salt, pink salt is not usually iodised.

How to keep fresh fruit fresh for longer

October 20262 min read

Fresh fruit starts changing the moment it is picked. A few simple habits at home make a real difference to how long it lasts.

Bananas

Keep them at room temperature until ripe. Bananas release a natural gas called ethylene that speeds up ripening, so keep them apart from other fruit unless you want everything to ripen faster. Once ripe, they can go in the fridge. The skin darkens, but the fruit inside stays good for a few more days.

Apples

Apples last longest in the fridge, where they can stay crisp for weeks. They also release ethylene, so give them their own drawer or bag if you can.

Grapes

Store grapes unwashed in the fridge, in the bag or box they came in. Wash them just before eating, because moisture left on the fruit makes it spoil sooner.

Three habits for every fruit

  • Do not wash fruit before storing it. Wash it just before you eat it.
  • Check the bowl every day and remove anything bruised or soft, because one bad piece affects the rest.
  • Overripe bananas freeze well for smoothies and baking.
